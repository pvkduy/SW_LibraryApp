- I controlled the style and scope of its output, for example asking it to stop over-explaining, to ask me before substituting its own decisions, and to cut the README down to setup-relevant content.

## 5. Assessment

**Where AI added real value**

- **Executing instead of assuming.** Running supposedly complete deliverables found 10 defects on each machine that reading the code would not have caught, including four successive Postgres migration failures diagnosed from evidence rather than guessed at.
- **Large-scale restructuring.** A 1458-line SRS was split into 21 files with FR/DEC/POL/NFR/TS/EC IDs preserved verbatim. Two duplicates slipped in and were caught by grep.
- **Adversarial review.** The 75-item checklist found real gaps (fine reconciliation across charges, queue behaviour when a book stops circulating, `held → lost` rules) that a read-through missed.
- **Cross-artifact consistency checks.** Stale sections, the Flask-vs-Supabase constitution, the `.specify` ignore rule, and filename mismatches were found by comparing files rather than trusting them.

**Where AI was unreliable**

- **Confident wrong claims.** "Speckit needs no install", "the project ref is a secret", and "local publishable keys aren't reproducible" were each stated flatly and were false. Each was corrected only after I pushed back or the claim was checked. My Firebase experience was a better guide in the credential discussion than the AI's initial framing.
- **Scope overreach.** It proposed a layout that violated a course requirement, ratified a constitution from partial inputs, and acted on its own decisions instead of asking.
- **Self-introduced regressions.** Duplicate FRs and a stale core section came from the AI's own edits and were found by its own follow-up checks.
- **Answer drift and over-explaining.** Several answers were generic or too dense and needed re-asking in simpler or more specific terms.
- **Untested tests.** One assertion was written that could not fail; only deliberately re-injecting the bug exposed it.

**Division of responsibility.** The AI was fast at execution and broad checking, but its outputs only became part of the project after I reviewed, challenged or directed them. Decisions about scope, design and course compliance stayed with me.

**Net effect.** The project moved from "files exist" to "files verified to execute": M0 (database, Edge Functions, browser tests, CI) is demonstrated working, and the Spec Kit package went from a single spec file and an empty constitution to a 21-file ID-preserving spec set with a quality review and two documented revision rounds. The recurring pattern was that the AI's first statement was often wrong and its second, after pushback, was right, so everything kept in this project was retained only after being checked against a file, a query, or a run.
