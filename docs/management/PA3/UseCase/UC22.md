## 1. Use-Case Name

**UC-22 - Assess damage and write off copies** (Traces to: `FEAT-22`, `FEAT-23`, `FEAT-24`, `FR-COND-01`, `FR-COND-02`, `FR-COND-03`, `FR-CIRC-05`, `FR-INV-05`, `FR-INV-07`, `POL-13`)

### 1.1 Brief Description

This use case enables a **Librarian (Admin)** to assess severe physical damage to books and process the formal decommissioning and retirement of inventory copies via Write-Off Slips. Library staff can inspect damaged, obsolete, or missing physical copies not currently in active circulation, assemble multi-copy write-off slips, attach mandatory photo evidence for destroyed items, and complete the write-off. Completing a slip permanently transitions the target copies to `retired` (or `lost`), writes immutable inventory audit transactions, decrements collection stock counters, and, if the retirement renders a title non-circulating, automatically handles outstanding reservation queues. It also enables staff to manually declare a borrowed copy as `lost`, enforcing loan lost penalties and replacement charges.

### 1.2 Actors

- **Primary Actor:** Librarian (Admin)
- **Supporting Actor:** Supabase Storage (Evidence photos bucket)

## 2. Flow of Events

### 2.1 Basic Flow

This use case begins when a **Librarian (Admin)** identifies physical copies in the library collection that are damaged beyond repair or confirmed destroyed, requiring formal decommissioning.

1. The **Librarian** navigates to the Write-Off Slips section at `/admin/writeoffs` and clicks "Create Write-Off Slip".
2. The **System** initializes a new slip in `draft` status, assigning a unique slip identifier (e.g., `WO-2026-0042`) and recording the creator (`librarian.id`).
3. The **Librarian** selects the formal write-off reason code:
   - `damaged_beyond_repair` (Physical destruction, severe water damage, mildew, missing pages)
   - `obsolete_edition` (Superseded curriculum materials discarded by policy)
   - `confirmed_missing` (Missing during shelf audit/stocktaking)
4. The **Librarian** adds target copies to the slip by scanning copy barcode labels or searching by book title.
5. For each added copy, the **System** verifies that the copy's current status is eligible (`available`, `damaged`, `under_repair`, or `lost`) and is NOT currently `borrowed` or `held`.
6. The **Librarian** specifies the target terminal status for each copy (`retired` for damaged/obsolete items, or `lost` for missing stock).
7. For copies flagged `damaged_beyond_repair`, the **Librarian** uploads mandatory photographic evidence (JPEG/PNG images displaying physical damage) to the `writeoff-evidence` storage bucket and enters a descriptive note.
8. The **Librarian** reviews the slip summary (total copies, replacement values, attached photos, notes) and clicks "Complete Slip".
9. The **System** executes an atomic server-side transaction that:
   - Validates that none of the copies became `borrowed` or `held` during editing.
   - Verifies that mandatory evidence photos exist for all `damaged_beyond_repair` items.
   - Updates the slip status from `draft` to `completed`, stamping `completed_at = now()`.
   - Transitions all listed copies to their target statuses (`retired` or `lost`).
   - Inserts an immutable `inventory_transactions` record for each copy with `type = write_off`, `delta = -1`, `reference_id = slip.id`, and `reason = slip.reason_code`.
   - Updates the book's denormalized counters (`total_copies = total_copies - 1`, and `available_copies` if previously available).
   - Checks if the book has zero remaining circulating copies and invokes [Extension Point 6.1](#61-queue-cancellation-on-non-circulating-title-transition) if needed.
10. The **System** displays a confirmation dialog stating that the write-off slip is completed and locked as immutable, providing a print/export option for audit compliance. The use case ends.

### 2.2 Alternative Flows

#### 2.2.1 Copy Blocked by Active Loan or Active Reservation Hold

_Branching point: At Step 4 or Step 9 of the Basic Flow, if an added copy is currently in status `borrowed` or `held`._

1. The **System** refuses to add or complete the copy on the slip.
2. The **System** highlights the conflicting copy and displays an explicit error message (`FR-COND-03`):
   - For `borrowed` copy: _"Copy [Barcode] is currently borrowed on loan [Loan ID]. On-loan copies cannot be written off via slip; process via loan Lost workflow."_
   - For `held` copy: _"Copy [Barcode] is currently held for reservation [Reservation ID]. Release or expire the hold before writing off."_
3. The **Librarian** removes the blocked copy from the slip (or releases the hold in a separate tab) and continues.
4. The flow resumes at Step 4 or Step 8 of the Basic Flow.

#### 2.2.2 Mandatory Photo Evidence Missing for Damaged-Beyond-Repair

_Branching point: At Step 8 of the Basic Flow, if a copy has reason `damaged_beyond_repair` but no evidence photos were uploaded._

1. The **System** blocks completion and highlights the photo upload section for the copy.
2. The **System** displays a validation error: _"At least one photographic evidence file is mandatory for reason code 'damaged_beyond_repair'."_
3. The **Librarian** takes or selects digital photos of the damaged physical copy and uploads them.
4. The flow resumes at Step 8 of the Basic Flow.

#### 2.2.3 Multi-Copy Write-Off Slip Creation and Draft Saving

_Branching point: At Step 7 of the Basic Flow, if the Librarian is compiling a large inventory write-off over multiple inspection sessions._

1. The **Librarian** enters partial copy barcodes, evidence photos, and notes.
2. The **Librarian** clicks "Save Draft".
3. The **System** persists the slip and its item lines in `draft` status without modifying any physical copy states or writing inventory transactions.
4. At a later time, the Librarian reopens `/admin/writeoffs`, selects the draft slip, edits items, and proceeds to Step 8 of the Basic Flow.

#### 2.2.4 Manual Mark as Lost for Copy on Active Loan

_Branching point: A Student notifies library staff that a borrowed book has been lost, or staff identifies an unreturned borrowed copy that must be written as lost._

1. The **Librarian** navigates to the active loan item in the Circulation portal (`/admin/circulation/loans/:id`).
2. The **Librarian** clicks "Mark Copy as Lost" (`FR-CIRC-05`).
3. The **System** presents a confirmation dialog outlining the contractual and policy consequences:
   - The loan item status transitions to `lost`.
   - The physical copy status transitions to `lost`.
   - The student's account status is immediately changed to `restricted` (`TS-28`).
   - Accrued overdue fines are frozen.
   - A `loss_fee` Charge equal to the book's full replacement price is generated.
4. The **Librarian** enters a mandatory reason (e.g., _"Student reported textbook left on campus bus"_) and confirms.
5. The **System** commits the lost transition in an atomic transaction, writes an `inventory_transactions` row (`type = lost`, `operator_id = librarian.id`), applies the restriction, creates the replacement fee, and notifies the student. The use case ends.

#### 2.2.5 Draft Slip Cancellation

_Branching point: The Librarian decides to abandon an incomplete draft write-off slip._

1. The **Librarian** opens an existing draft slip and clicks "Cancel Slip".
2. The **System** prompts for confirmation.
3. Upon confirmation, the **System** transitions the slip status to `cancelled`. No changes are applied to copies, and no inventory transactions are written. The use case ends.

## 3. Special Requirements

### 3.1 Strict Immutability and Audit Logging

- Completed write-off slips are completely immutable. The application UI hides all edit and delete controls, and server-side database triggers prevent any `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on completed slips (`FR-COND-03`).
- Every decommissioned copy must generate a matching immutable row in `inventory_transactions` referencing the slip ID, preserving full provenance for university audit and asset depreciation reports (`FR-INV-05`).

### 3.2 No Hard Deletion Policy

- Physical copies, book titles, and write-off records must never be hard-deleted from PostgreSQL (`POL-13`). Retired items retain their complete historical records, barcodes, and loan histories for institutional accountability.

### 3.3 Non-Circulating Queue Cancellation Rules

- If a write-off slip decommissions the last circulating copy of a title such that total usable copies becomes zero, the transaction must execute `POL-07`: any pending reservation queues for that title are cancelled with notification to students rather than being orphaned in the system (`POL-13`).

### 3.4 Role-Based Access Control

- Only users with verified `admin` role can create, edit, or complete write-off slips (`FR-AUTH-05`). Student roles have no read or write access to write-off endpoints.

## 4. Preconditions

### 4.1 Administrative Authentication

The Librarian is logged into the administrative portal with an active session holding inventory management privileges.

### 4.2 Copy Eligibility

Target physical copies to be included in a write-off slip exist in the system and are not in `borrowed` or `held` circulation states.

---

## 5. Postconditions

### 5.1 Slip Completed and Copies Decommissioned

- The write-off slip status is permanently set to `completed`.
- Listed physical copies transition to `retired` (or `lost`).
- Inventory transactions (`type = write_off`) are committed.
- Book catalog counters (`total_copies`, `available_copies`) are decremented.
- Supporting evidence photos are securely retained in storage.

### 5.2 Write-Off Cancelled or Saved as Draft

- If draft is saved, copy statuses remain unchanged.
- If cancelled, the slip is marked `cancelled` without inventory changes.

---

## 6. Extension Points

### 6.1 Queue Cancellation on Non-Circulating Title Transition

- **Location:** Step 9 of Basic Flow.
- **Trigger:** When completing a write-off leaves a book title with zero circulating copies while reservation queues exist.
- **Behavior:** The system triggers `POL-07`, systematically cancelling all waiting reservations for the book, sending cancellation notification emails to affected students, and updating queue metrics.
