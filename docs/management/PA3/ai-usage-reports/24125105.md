## 2. My Role and Involvement

I drove the design and execution of all deliverables under my responsibility. The AI acted strictly as an assistant operating within explicitly defined constraints; all business policies, domain boundaries, and final text edits were decided and owned by me.

### What I Owned and Decided

- **Functional Scope & Allocation:** I determined the architectural boundary between Student-facing services and Staff/Librarian administration. I authored the core identity use cases (UC-01–UC-04) and all back-office operational use cases (UC-19–UC-32), ensuring full coverage of library administration.
- **Business Rules & State Transitions:** I defined the operational conditions for book write-offs, damage assessments, lost-book penalties, 90-day refund windows for recovered lost books, and clearance prerequisites for student account deactivation/restoration.
- **RUP Template Enforcement:** I mandated strict adherence to the 6 core RUP sections (Revision History, Table of Contents, 1. Use-Case Name/Actors, 2. Flow of Events with Basic and Alternative Flows, 3. Special Requirements, 4. Preconditions, 5. Postconditions, 6. Extension Points) across all documents.
- **Traceability to Architecture:** I ensured all use cases correctly reference underlying PostgreSQL tables (`profiles`, `books`, `inventory_transactions`, `charges`, `receipts`, `write_off_slips`), Row-Level Security (RLS) constraints, and frontend routes.

### How I Checked and Corrected the AI

- **Step Resumption Accuracy:** In branched alternative flows (e.g., input validation failures, draft slip cancellations, batch modifications), the AI frequently suggested generic "flow ends" or incorrect step references. I systematically audited every alternative flow to specify exact resumption points (e.g., correcting resumption from Step 5 to Step 4 in UC-04; adding dual resumption options to Step 11 or 15 in UC-24).
- **Actor Realignment:** When reviewing the Use-Case Catalog and specifications, I detected that UC-04 (Manage personal profile) and UC-28 (View notifications) were mistakenly scoped only to Students; I updated the catalog and flows to include `Librarian (Admin)` as a primary actor since staff also manage profiles and receive administrative alerts.
- **Route and Entity Consistency:** I corrected route assumptions generated by the AI to match our project conventions (e.g., updating `/admin/acquisitions/:id` to `/admin/batches/:id` in UC-26).
- **Cross-Use-Case Linkage:** In commit `270e746` and `e967e1e`, I added explicit cross-references between related use cases that the AI initially kept isolated (e.g., linking UC-22 copy damage/loss to UC-18 lost-book resolution; linking UC-31 account termination to UC-23 negative reversal receipts).

### What I Delegated to the AI

- Generating the standard Markdown skeleton matching the RUP Word template for rapid authoring.
- Brainstorming comprehensive alternative flows and edge-case scenarios (e.g., token expiration, duplicate student ID conflicts, partial barcode match, batch order version immutability).
- Formulating non-functional requirements and accessibility constraints (WCAG 2.1 AA, latency targets, RLS policies) tailored to each specific use case.
- Auditing the entire PA3 repository against the official course requirements.

## 5. Assessment

### Where AI Added Real Value

- **RUP Structural Consistency:** Maintaining 100% syntactic and structural consistency across 32 individual Markdown files (each with 6 numbered sections, revision tables, and TOCs) is highly labor-intensive; the AI excelled at enforcing this template without skipping sections.
- **Exhaustive Alternative Flows:** The AI assisted in brainstorming realistic exception scenarios that could easily be overlooked during manual writing (e.g., student ID collisions, token expirations, 90-day refund window cutoffs, barcode camera hardware failures).
- **Traceability Verification:** It rapidly cross-referenced use cases with underlying functional requirements (`FEAT-xx`), architectural principles, and Supabase tables.

### Where AI Was Unreliable / Limitations

- **Branch Resumption Numbering:** The AI frequently hallucinated incorrect step resumption numbers (e.g., writing "resumes at Step 5" when the basic flow step was actually Step 4), requiring diligent line-by-line manual verification.
- **Syllabus-Specific Constraints:** The AI drafted text specifications thoroughly but did not proactively warn about external physical deliverables (such as the mandatory requirement for embedded UI prototype screenshots under each UC specification) until prompted to cross-check directly against the assignment grading rubric.
- **Domain Nuance:** The AI occasionally defaulted to generic e-commerce checkout patterns rather than our university library policies (e.g., forgetting that memberships cannot be transferred, or that lost book replacement fees involve an admin write-off transaction).
