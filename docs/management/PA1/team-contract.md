# **Team Contract**

## **2. Communication Plan**

**Communication tools and meetings**

- The primary communication channel is Messenger.  
- Meetings are held either in person or online via Google Meet.  
- Sprint backlog and task completion managed on Jira

**Response expectations**

- Every member must react to or reply to important announcements or major group agreements to confirm that they have read and understood the information.  
- A member who cannot attend a meeting or cannot complete assigned work must notify the team promptly, rather than staying silent.  
- Members must proactively check for assigned tasks on Jira and update their progress

**Decision-making and conflict-resolution protocol**

- Sensitive or interpersonal matters, such as serious disagreements about a member's work ethic, attitude, conduct, or task allocation, should be discussed face to face (in person or in a video call), not over text chat to avoid misunderstandings. If direct discussion does not resolve the matter, the escalation steps in Section 7 apply.   
- Formal decision-making and conflict-resolution procedures are defined in Sections 6 and 7.

## **3. Work Schedule and Deadlines**

**Meeting schedule**

- The team holds at most two meetings per week. Additional meetings may be called only when the team agrees by majority vote.   
- Each Sprint (synonymous to a PA) includes one Sprint Planning meeting, two Scrum meetings spread across the Sprint, and one Sprint Review meeting.

**Internal deadlines**

- Each member completes their task two days before the submission deadline (three days before for large tasks) so that the team has time to review and make corrections.  
- Deadlines must not be missed unless notified with the team beforehand and accepted.

**Contingency plan for delays**

- A member who expects to miss a deadline must report it to the team immediately.  
- The team will then redistribute work, and other members will step in to help complete the delayed task.

## **4. Code and Documentation Standards**

**Version control**

- Commit messages must be meaningful and describe the change made.  
- When new commits appear on `main` and do not cause serious conflicts, members should proactively pull them into their working branch.  
- If a member needs to make significant changes (especially deleting) to code written by another member, they must notify that member beforehand. Minor or unrelated code that functions fine should be left untouched.  
- A pull request into `main` is made at the end of each Project Assignment (Sprint).

**Security**

- Files containing API keys or credentials must be listed in `.gitignore`. A placeholder version of such a file (e.g., `google-services.json`) is committed instead.  
- API keys, secrets, and credentials must never be committed to the repository.

**Documentation**

- Members proactively write usage instructions and update the `README` whenever there is a major change or a new service is introduced.  
- All documents are written in English using Markdown, with diagrams in Mermaid where possible, and are stored under `/docs` in the Git repository.  
- Each document section states who performed, reviewed, and edited it.

**Review and testing**

Completed work is checked by teammates before the deadline (see Section 3), and the Tester verifies completed features against the test cases.

## **5. Accountability and Performance**

**Expectations**

- Each member completes the tasks assigned and agreed upon during team meetings.  
- Every task is logged on the task board, assigned to exactly one member, and marked as done only when the work is actually finished.  
- A member who considers the workload unfair or an assignment unsuitable is expected to speak up.  
- If submitted work does not meet the expected quality, the member will be asked to redo it.

**Consequences**

If a member repeatedly fails to meet expectations and shows no willingness to improve, that member's recognized contribution percentage will be reduced in the team reports.

## **6. Decision-Making Process**

Team decisions are made by majority vote.

## **7. Conflict Resolution**

Conflicts are handled in three escalating steps:

1. **Between the members involved:** any two members resolve the disagreement directly with each other, provided it does not affect project progress.  
2. **Third-member mediation:** if the issue grows larger, a third team member is brought in to help resolve it.  
3. **Escalation:** if the conflict cannot be resolved within the team, it is reported to the TA or instructor for resolution.

## **8. Review and Update Process**

- If a member finds any clause unreasonable, they raise it in a team meeting.  
- The contract is amended only when a majority vote at the meeting approves the change.
